# Sample alerts, foreclosed

> Skipped, and the distinction matters: not a cost preference, but an exercise
> a previously committed decision makes unreachable.

## What was configured

Nothing. No Defender plan was enabled and no sample alerts were generated.

## What was established

Sample alerts are generated by selecting the **Defender plans** to generate
for, and the alerts exist only where a plan is enabled on the subscription.
`POS-033` records Defender for Cloud **declined at Lab 04**, and names the
consequence this project has been paying since: no Defender for Servers P2,
therefore no 500 MB/day/server `SecurityEvent` allowance, therefore the DCR
tier as the only available cost lever.

So the exercise's prerequisite is absent by a decision already on the record.
Reversing it to run one exercise would have opened a **second meter on a
different billing basis** inside a paid clock hour.

This is the second instance in two sessions of a committed finding
**pre-refuting a later guide's advice** — `POS-058` did the same to the before-allocating-SCUs note's
Azure-credit escape hatch. Worth naming as a pattern: a repository that records
its refusals accumulates the ability to answer later questions without
re-testing them.

## What was corrected

Nothing shipped from this walkthrough and was disproved.

## What could not be tested

**The sample-alert exercise was never load-bearing, and that is the useful part.** The subsequent Copilot investigation
requires an *incident ID*, not Microsoft's synthetic alerts. Lab 20 pointed
Copilot at **incident 24** — Lab 19's, already documented across seven surfaces
with its portal defects recorded.

That substitution is why the lab produced a measurement rather than a
walkthrough. Synthetic alerts have no committed ground truth to score an answer
against; incident 24 does. The skip improved the lab.

Untested here: whether sample alerts differ from real detections in any way
Copilot can perceive. Unanswerable in this tenant.
